## Chapter 4: The Dusty Track and Rivalry Begins

***Friday, February 7th***

***You can feel the buzz in the air today. It's Athletics Day at Low Water Primary, and every kids got that look of pure excitement. Well, almost every kid. Me? I can't see what's so special about a bunch of white lines on sandy dirt. Mr. Jones and the Grade 6 class spent all of Friday getting the track ready, and I still don't get it. It's just dust with boundaries.***

[Illustration suggestion: Abdullah looking unimpressed at the track while other kids around him are buzzing with excitement.]

***I've always been decent at sports, so I'm cautiously optimistic about my chances today. But there's this guy, Richard—Brians best bud—tall, lean, and a stamina machine. He's been the school's track hero since forever, and everyone expects him to sweep the long distance again.***

***The day kicks off early, and surprise, surprise—Richards taking gold in the 1500m and 800m. But as the distances get shorter, I start closing the gap. By the time the 400m comes around, I'm right on his heels, and the crowds starting to take notice.***

[Illustration suggestion: Richard crossing the finish line with Abdullah just a step behind, both panting and sweaty.]

***Sprints are my jam. I can feel the power in my legs, the kind of muscle you get from chasing goats, not from running in circles. So when the 200m starts, and I leave everyone in the dust, Richards smile starts to wobble. The cheers for me are getting louder, and the look on his face tells me he didnt see this coming.***

[Illustration suggestion: Abdullah surging ahead in the 200m sprint, with a crowd of students cheering wildly in the background.]

***When the 100m dash comes around, I'm ready. I explode off the line, and it's like I'm back home, running through the fields, free and unstoppable. I cross the finish line first, and it feels like flying.***

[Illustration suggestion: Abdullah in full sprint, a blur to the onlookers, crossing the finish line with a clear lead.]

***Richards not clapping, though. When I offer my hand in a sportsmanlike shake, he just stares at it like it's a dead fish. I retract my hand, a little sting of disappointment hitting me, but I brush it off. It's just a race, right?***

***Then there's Berenice, Richards girl—or at least, that's what everyone thought. She's over here, jumping up and down, and I swear she's more excited about my win than I am. She throws her arms around me, and I can feel every pair of eyes on the field burning into my back, especially Richards.***

[Illustration suggestion: Berenice hugging Abdullah, who looks surprised and awkward, as Richard glares at them from the background.]

***That's when I feel it—the shift. It's not just about a race anymore. It's not just about who's the fastest. It's something more, something deeper, and I can tell this is only the beginning. Richards face is hard, his eyes cold, and I know that whatever this year was going to be, it just got a lot more complicated.***

[Illustration suggestion: Richards face, twisted in a mix of anger and jealousy, watching Abdullah and Berenice celebrating.]

***As the day winds down and the dust settles on the makeshift track, I realize that I've won more than just a race today. I've won the attention I wasnt sure I wanted, made a rival I didnt mean to make, and somehow, I've stepped into a war that I didnt even know was being fought.***

[Illustration suggestion: Abdullah sitting alone on the bleachers, looking pensive as he watches the deserted track, the setting sun casting long shadows.]

***But one things for sure: I'm not backing down. This is my life now, and I'm going to live it on my terms, running full speed ahead. Bring it on, Richard. I'm just getting started.***
