## Chapter 8: The Final Conflict

**Friday, February 21st**

The air was thick with anticipation. Every student in the school was lined up in neat rows on the dusty patch of earth in front of the main building. The sun was high, and I could feel it's heat pressing down on us like a weight. It matched the heaviness in my chest as we stood for the weekly assembly.

Mr. Jones was at the front, pacing slowly as he gave his usual speech about discipline and "making better choices." But his words barely registered with me. I could feel my heart pounding in my ears. Everything that had happened over the past few weeks—the teasing, the rivalry with Richard, the latrine disaster, Berenice snapping at Richard—it all seemed to be leading to this moment.

I glanced over at Richard, who was standing a few rows away. His face was unreadable, but his fists were clenched at his sides. Ever since Berenice had shut him down in front of everyone, he’d been keeping his distance. But I could feel the anger radiating off him, simmering just beneath the surface. And today, something felt different. The tension between us was like a live wire, buzzing and ready to snap.

"…and that’s why it’s important," Mr. Jones’ voice rang out, "that each of you takes responsibility for your actions."

His eyes swept over the crowd, pausing for a moment on me and Richard. It was clear the message was meant for us, though he didn’t say it outright.

Suddenly, a loud whistle cut through the silence, drawing everyone’s attention. I turned to see Morgan, one of the boys from our class, nudging Brian and pointing at Dora, who was standing just a few rows ahead of us. She was barefoot, her long, sharp toenails digging into the dirt like talons. It was the same joke they’d been making since Grade 3\.

Morgan started whistling again, louder this time, and Richard smirked, egging him on.

"Careful, Brian," Morgan said, just loud enough for a few others to hear. "She might scratch you next!"

The boys nearby burst into laughter. I glanced at Dora. Her face flushed red with anger and embarrassment, but she didn’t say anything. She just glared at Morgan, fists clenched at her sides.

The laughter grew louder, and soon, other students were joining in. Mr. Jones, standing at the front, was too busy talking to notice what was happening. But I saw it. I saw the moment when Dora had enough.

With a quick, angry movement, she swung her foot out and scratched Morgan’s calf with her long toenails. He yelped in pain, stumbling backward as blood trickled down his leg.

The laughter stopped. Everything froze for a second as the entire assembly fell silent. Mr. Jones spun around, eyes narrowed, as he tried to figure out what had just happened.

"WHAT is going on here?" Mr. Jones’ voice thundered through the silence.

Morgan clutched his leg, still grimacing, and pointed accusingly at Dora. "She scratched me!"

Mr. Jones’ eyes scanned the crowd, and in an instant, his gaze landed on me. It didn’t matter that I hadn’t done anything. It never did.

"Abdullah!" he snapped, his voice sharp. "And Richard! Both of you—outside the office. Now!"

I blinked in shock. "But I didn’t—"

"NOW!" Mr. Jones cut me off, his tone leaving no room for argument.

I could feel the eyes of the entire school on me as I stepped out of line, my heart sinking. Richard followed behind me, his face set in a hard, defiant expression. This wasn’t just about Dora and Morgan. Mr. Jones was making an example out of us.

---


**Outside Mr. Jones’ Office**

The bench outside Mr. Jones’ office had never felt more uncomfortable. I sat there, my hands trembling slightly as I rested them in my lap, waiting for the inevitable punishment. Richard sat beside me, arms crossed, staring straight ahead with a scowl on his face.

The other boys—Morgan, Francois, Gerald—had already been called into the office. I could hear the sharp crack of the cane through the door, followed by muffled whimpers of pain. Each time the door opened, another boy came out, face red and eyes watery, and then disappeared down the hall. I knew it was only a matter of time before it was my turn.

The door opened again, and Francois emerged, rubbing his backside with a grimace. Mr. Jones’ voice followed him out. "Next!"

My stomach dropped. This was it. I braced myself, pushing off the bench to stand. But before I could move, I heard Richard mutter something under his breath.

"You shouldn’t even be here," he said, his voice low and full of resentment.

I froze. "What did you just say?"

Richard finally turned to look at me, his eyes cold. "You don’t belong here. You should’ve stayed in Congo."

The words hit me like a punch to the gut. My heart pounded, and suddenly, everything I had been holding in—the anger, the frustration, the loneliness—boiled over.

I took a step toward him, fists clenched at my sides. "What did you say?" I asked again, my voice shaking with barely contained fury.

"You heard me," Richard said, standing up to face me. "You think you’re so great because you can run fast? Because Berenice likes you? You don’t belong here. You’ll never belong here."

For a moment, everything blurred. I could feel the anger coursing through me, begging me to lash out. All the humiliation, all the teasing, the taunts, the rivalry—it all came rushing to the surface. My fists tightened, and I could feel the heat rising in my chest.

I was done.

I took another step closer, my body trembling with the urge to hit him. To show him that I wasn’t the weak, scared new kid he thought I was.

But then something stopped me.

A voice—my mother’s voice—echoed in my mind. The stories she used to tell me when I was little. About how, no matter what happened, no matter how much people tried to bring us down, we had to rise above it. To be better.

I froze, fists still clenched, staring at Richard. His face twisted into a sneer, like he was daring me to hit him, to take the bait.

But I didn’t. I couldn’t.

Because hitting him wouldn’t make me feel better. It wouldn’t fix anything. It wouldn’t change the fact that I missed my parents. That I was far from home. That I was still trying to find my place in this new world.

It wouldn’t change who I was.

So I did the hardest thing I could think of.

I stepped back.

Richard’s sneer faltered. "What? Too scared?"

I shook my head, my fists slowly unclenching. "No. I’m not scared," I said, my voice steady now. "I just don’t need to prove anything to you."

The door to Mr. Jones’ office swung open, and he stepped out, cane in hand, glaring at the two of us. "Abdullah, Richard. Inside. Now."

I took a deep breath, my heart still racing, but I felt lighter somehow. Like a weight had been lifted. I had made my choice.

As we stepped into the office, I knew there would be consequences. I would face the punishment, just like the others. But this time, it was different. This time, I wasn’t running from who I was.

And for the first time since I arrived in South Africa, I felt like I was finally standing on solid ground.
