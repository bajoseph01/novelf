## Chapter 7: Consequences and Reflection

---


**Tuesday, February 18th**

The next morning, walking to school felt like marching to my own funeral. The sun was out, the air was warm, but inside, I was cold, tense. My feet dragged along the dirt road, kicking up little clouds of dust as I went. All I could think about was the disaster that unfolded yesterday—the latrine spill, Mr. Miyagi standing there, half-covered in sewage, and the laughter of the other kids echoing around me.

This was it. There was no coming back from this. My rivalry with Richard had reached a boiling point, and the whole school knew about it. The fact that I had to face everyone again after what happened made my stomach twist. I dreaded every step that brought me closer to Low Water Primary.

As I walked through the school gates, I could already feel eyes on me. Whispers. Snickers. My shoulders tensed, and I tried to block it all out, heading straight for the classroom. I could already see Richard up ahead, standing with his usual group of friends. Brian was nearby, nervously fiddling with his shiny hair while Gerald and Mervin were talking about something I couldn’t quite catch.

But it wasn’t long before the laughter started.

---


**In the Classroom**

I walked into the classroom, and instantly, the whispers started swirling around me like flies. Kids giggled and nudged each other as I passed by. I could hear snippets of their conversations, but I tried to block it out.

"Did you see him yesterday?"
"How does anyone recover from that?"
"I’m surprised he even showed up."

I slumped down into my seat, trying to make myself as small as possible. Berenice was already at her desk, writing something in her notebook. When she saw me, she flashed a small smile, but I barely managed to return it.

She leaned over. "Don’t let them get to you, Abdullah. They’ll forget about it soon enough."

I appreciated the attempt, but I wasn’t so sure. The incident was too big, too gross, to just fade away like that.

Just as I was settling in, I noticed Richard enter the room. He had that smug look on his face that made my blood boil. He might have been covered in the same filth as me yesterday, but somehow, he always managed to come out looking like the victim. He strolled in like nothing had happened, as if the whole thing had been my fault.

And then, out of nowhere, the teasing took a turn.

"You and Richard look like such a cute couple, Dullah," one of the boys called out from the back of the room, setting off a ripple of laughter.

I clenched my fists under the desk, the anger rising. Richard’s eyes locked onto mine, and the smirk on his face only grew wider.

"Leave him alone, Gerald," Berenice’s voice rang out across the room, sharper than usual.

Suddenly, the laughter stopped.

She stood up, her chair scraping loudly against the floor. I hadn’t seen her this angry before. She marched right over to where Richard was standing with Gerald and the other boys who’d been teasing me.

"Richard, I’m sick of it," Berenice snapped. Her face was flushed, and she jabbed a finger at his chest. "I’m sick of you and your little group treating me like I’m your girlfriend. I am not your girlfriend, I never was your girlfriend—"

The whole classroom was dead silent now. Richard’s face paled, the smug look slipping away as Berenice tore into him.

"—and I am nobody’s girlfriend right now. Leave me alone to like who I like and spend time with who I choose!"

The finality in her words hit Richard like a slap across the face. His jaw clenched, and for a moment, it looked like he was going to say something. But instead, he just turned away, his fists tight by his sides.

The classroom erupted into whispers again, but this time, they weren’t about me. They were about Berenice’s outburst. She had just publicly shut Richard down in front of everyone, and I could tell by the look on his face that he wasn’t going to forget it anytime soon.

---


**Facing Uncle Jan**

That afternoon, the humiliation still hung heavy on my shoulders as I trudged home. The whole day had been a disaster. Despite Berenice’s defense, the teasing continued. The whispers and the laughter followed me everywhere, no matter how much I tried to ignore it.

But worse than that was the thought of what awaited me at home.

When I reached the farm, Uncle Jan was already waiting for me, standing by the goat pen with his arms crossed and a sour look on his face. My stomach sank. I knew the news of the latrine disaster had made it's way to him. There was no escaping the consequences now.

"You got anything to say for yourself?" he asked, his voice cold and sharp as a whip.

I swallowed hard. "It wasn’t... it wasn’t just me."

Uncle Jan’s eyes narrowed. "Oh, I know it wasn’t just you. But you’re the one standing in front of me right now, aren’t you?"

I couldn’t argue with that. I looked down at my feet, unable to meet his gaze.

"Do you have any idea what kind of trouble you’ve caused? What kind of embarrassment?" he continued, his voice steady but full of disappointment. "You’re supposed to represent this family here. And what have you done instead? You’ve made yourself a joke."

Each word cut deeper than the last. I’d rather he yelled at me, anything but this cold, quiet anger. I felt like a complete failure.

"I’ve already had a word with Mr. Jones," Uncle Jan added, crossing his arms tighter. "You’re lucky you didn’t get suspended. But from now on, you’ll be working extra hours on the farm, and you better get used to it. If you’ve got energy to cause trouble at school, you’ve got energy to do more work here."

There was nothing I could say. I nodded, knowing there was no room for negotiation. He turned away, and I knew the conversation was over.

---


**Reflection**

That evening, I stood in the goat pen, my hands raw from pulling weeds and feeding the stubborn animals. The sun was setting, casting long shadows over the farm, but the heaviness in my chest didn’t fade with the light.

I leaned against the fence, wiping the sweat from my brow, and stared out at the horizon. My mind kept replaying the last few days, trying to make sense of how things had spiraled out of control so quickly. The rivalry with Richard, the constant teasing, the humiliation—none of it seemed to end.

I thought about what Berenice had said earlier. The way she stood up to Richard, made it clear she wasn’t going to let him control her or decide who she could be friends with. For a moment, I admired her strength. But then I realized something—Berenice had stood up for herself. I hadn’t. I had let Richard’s taunts, his arrogance, get under my skin and push me into one mess after another.

And now, I was paying the price.

---


**Setting the Stage**

The next day at school, the tension was palpable. Richard wasn’t speaking to anyone. He avoided me at every turn, but I could feel his anger simmering just below the surface.

The teasing hadn’t stopped, but it had changed. People were now more interested in Berenice’s outburst than in the latrine disaster, which was both a relief and a new source of tension. Richard’s pride had taken a hit, and I knew it wasn’t over. He would find a way to make me pay for everything that had happened—especially for what Berenice had said.

By the time lunchtime rolled around, I felt the weight of the whole school watching us, waiting for something to happen. Berenice had been keeping her distance, and Brian—well, he was Brian, still making jokes, still trying to pretend like everything was fine. But it wasn’t.

And then, as if the universe was setting us up for another disaster, Mr. Jones made an announcement at the end of the day.

"There will be an important assembly at the end of the week," he said, his voice carrying over the chatter of the students. "I expect all of you to be on your best behavior."

Assembly. That meant everyone in one place. And somehow, I just knew it was going to be another disaster waiting to happen. The tension between me and Richard had reached it's boiling point.

Something was going to give.

**End of Chapter 7**
