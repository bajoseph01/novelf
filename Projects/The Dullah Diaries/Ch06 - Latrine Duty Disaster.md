## Chapter 6: Latrine Duty Disaster

**Monday, February 17th**

Standing in Mr. Jones’ office, I had never been more aware of how much trouble I was in. My feet felt like they were glued to the floor, and I could feel Richard shifting next to me, arms crossed, still fuming. Mr. Jones sat at his desk, staring us down like we were bugs under a magnifying glass.

"Disrupting choir practice, knocking each other around, and turning the room into a circus," Mr. Jones began, his voice low and controlled, which somehow made it even scarier. "What exactly do you two think you're doing?"

Neither of us answered. There wasn’t much to say that wouldn’t make things worse. My mind raced as I tried to come up with something—anything—that could explain why we’d acted like idiots in choir. But before I could even open my mouth, Mr. Jones cut in.

"I don’t want excuses," he snapped. "I want solutions. Clearly, you two have a problem. And since you can’t solve it on your own, we’ll give you something to work on together."

My stomach dropped. That didn’t sound good.

"Starting today," Mr. Jones continued, "you’ll be helping Mr. Hendricks with latrine duty for the rest of the week. After school. Every day."

Latrine duty. The words hung in the air like the smell of the farm's worst goat pen. I didn’t know what was worse—the idea of cleaning out the school’s pit toilets, or the fact that I had to do it with Richard.

Mr. Jones must’ve seen the horror on my face, because he leaned back in his chair and smiled—a thin, humorless smile. "Maybe some time spent doing real work will help you two figure out how to get along."

Before I could protest, Richard spoke up. "Whatever. Fine."

That was all Mr. Jones needed to hear. "You’re dismissed," he said, waving his hand toward the door. "Meet Mr. Hendricks after school. He’ll show you what to do."

We shuffled out of the office without a word. My stomach was doing backflips, but Richard’s face was set in stone, as if this whole thing didn’t bother him at all. I, on the other hand, couldn’t help but feel like I was walking into some kind of nightmare.

---


**Later that Afternoon**

The bell rang, signaling the end of the school day. Normally, that sound was music to my ears, but today it felt like the tolling of doom. I made my way over to the janitor’s shed behind the school, where I found Richard already waiting, leaning against the door with his arms crossed and a scowl on his face.

Mr. Hendricks, the school janitor, appeared a minute later. He was a short, grizzled man with thick glasses that made his eyes look twice as big as they should be. He gave us both a look that clearly said he wasn’t impressed.

"So, you two are the new recruits, huh?" he said, scratching his scruffy chin. "Well, let’s not waste time. Follow me."

He led us around the back of the school to the pit toilets—six squat, rundown outhouses that made the goat pens at Uncle Jan’s farm look like a five-star hotel. The smell hit me before we even got close, and I had to fight the urge to gag. Richard didn’t flinch, but I noticed him wrinkle his nose.

"Here’s how it works," Mr. Hendricks said, gesturing toward the outhouses with a grim smile. "Each of these toilets has a bucket underneath. You’ll be emptying them into these larger bins." He pointed to two massive metal containers on wheels. "Once the bins are full, you’ll wheel them down to the sewage tank at the bottom of the hill."

I could already see where this was going, and I didn’t like it one bit.

"Any questions?" Mr. Hendricks asked, not that he really cared. "No? Good. Get to work."

He tossed us each a pair of gloves and bandanas to tie over our faces, but it didn’t do much to block out the stench. I pulled the bandana over my nose and got to work, gagging slightly as I opened the first toilet and saw the disgusting sight waiting for me. Richard, to his credit, didn’t complain. He just started working in silence.

It didn’t take long before the first of the big bins was full. Richard and I each grabbed a handle, lifting the heavy bin and struggling to get it onto the wheelbarrows Mr. Hendricks had left for us. The wheels creaked as we pushed the barrows toward the steep hill behind the school.

"We’ve gotta be careful," I muttered, mostly to myself. "If we go too fast—"

"Relax," Richard interrupted. "It’s not that hard. We just have to get this to the bottom. Piece of cake."

I shot him a look but didn’t argue. The hill was steep, and the wheelbarrow felt like it weighed a ton. As we started to descend, I could feel the weight pulling me forward. I had to slow my steps to keep control.

Richard, of course, saw this as a challenge.

"Come on, Dullah, stop dragging your feet," he said with a smirk. Then, without warning, he picked up speed.

"Don’t!" I warned, but it was too late.

Richard was already racing ahead, pushing the wheelbarrow faster and faster down the hill. I gritted my teeth and tried to keep up, my hands gripping the handles tightly as the wheelbarrow began to wobble under the pressure. The sloshing sounds of the waste inside made me feel sick.

"Richard, slow down!" I shouted, but he just laughed and kept going.

I could hear Brian and Gerald standing at the top of the hill, shouting warnings and laughing as we tore down the slope. My heart was pounding, and all I could think about was how this was going to end badly.

And then it happened.

Richard’s wheelbarrow hit a rock, tipping sideways and spilling it's contents. I barely had time to react before mine followed suit. Both wheelbarrows overturned, sending a wave of foul-smelling liquid flying through the air. The next thing I knew, I was on the ground, covered in waste, my wheelbarrow on it's side.

But that wasn’t the worst part.

As I wiped the disgusting mess from my face, I saw it—*the new blue school minibus*. Parked at the bottom of the hill, and now covered in the sewage we’d just spilled. And standing next to it, looking horrified, was Mr. Miyagi.

I froze. Richard was on the ground next to me, looking equally stunned. The silence was deafening as the reality of what had just happened sank in. The school’s brand-new minibus—the one they’d only just gotten—was ruined. And Mr. Miyagi, our principal, was standing there, half-covered in sewage himself, his face a mask of shock.

"Oh no," I whispered, my voice barely audible.

"Oh yes," Mr. Miyagi said, his voice shaking with barely-contained fury.

Richard groaned, rolling over and staring up at the mess we’d made. "This... this is bad."

"Yeah," I said, wiping my face again. "Really bad."

Mr. Jones appeared a few moments later, his eyes wide as he took in the scene. He looked at me, then at Richard, then at the minibus, and finally at Mr. Miyagi, who was still standing in stunned silence.

"What... what happened here?" Mr. Jones asked, his voice low and dangerous.

Neither Richard nor I said a word. There wasn’t much we could say. The evidence was all around us.

Mr. Jones let out a long sigh, rubbing his temples as if trying to stave off a massive headache. "I don’t even know where to begin," he muttered.

I wanted to disappear. I wanted to be anywhere but here. Covered in waste, staring at the ruined school bus, I felt like this was rock bottom. And as much as I wanted to blame Richard, I knew we were both equally responsible for this disaster.

"You two," Mr. Jones finally said, his voice calm but firm. "Go clean yourselves up. We’ll deal with this later."

He didn’t have to tell us twice. Richard and I scrambled to our feet, still reeking of sewage, and hurried away from the scene, the laughter of the other students echoing behind us.

---


**Later that Evening**

As I trudged home, still smelling faintly of the day’s disaster despite scrubbing myself clean, all I could think about was how much trouble I was in. Not just with the school, but with Uncle Jan. There was no way this wouldn’t get back to him. And after today, I knew I’d have to face more than just Mr. Jones’ anger.

The worst part? I wasn’t even mad at Richard anymore. I was just exhausted. Exhausted from trying to keep up with him, exhausted from constantly getting into trouble, and exhausted from being the new kid who couldn’t seem to catch a break.

As I reached Uncle Jan’s farm, the goats bleating their usual greetings, I took a deep breath. Tomorrow was going to be worse—I could feel it. But for now, all I could do was collapse into bed and hope that somehow, someway, this whole mess would blow over.

But deep down, I knew it wouldn’t. This rivalry with Richard was far from over.

**End of Chapter 6**
