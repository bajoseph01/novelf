## Chapter 9: Abdullah Snaps and Runs

---


Abdullah’s fists clenched at his sides as he stood over Richard, every muscle in his body tense. Richard was on the ground, smirking despite everything, egging him on with a look that seemed to say, *Go ahead, hit me.* The words Richard had spat out—telling Abdullah he didn’t belong—echoed in his mind, fueling his anger. His knuckles tightened, and for a second, he thought about it. About giving in, letting his fist connect with Richard’s face. Maybe then it would end. Maybe then Richard would understand.

But just as quickly, he stopped himself. He wasn’t going to give Richard the satisfaction. Not like this. Not here.

His breathing slowed, and he took a step back, loosening his fists. He wouldn’t do it.

Before he could make sense of the emotions swirling inside him, the door to Mr. Jones’ office swung open. Mr. Jones stepped out, his eyes hard as they locked onto Abdullah. "Abdullah!" he barked, voice slicing through the tension. "If you force me to come get you, you’ll regret it."

Abdullah’s chest tightened. He knew that tone, that threat. He had heard it too many times before. He could feel the weight of Mr. Jones’ authority pressing down on him, trapping him. And in that moment, something snapped.

He wasn’t going to get hit for something he didn’t even do. Not again.

The walls of the school office felt like they were closing in around him as Mr. Jones grabbed his shoulder, steering him toward the door. The other boys stood by, watching, waiting to see what would happen next. Richard climbed to his feet, still sneering, and the smirk on his face was the last straw.

Abdullah yanked his arm free from Mr. Jones’ grip and bolted.

"Abdullah!" Mr. Jones’ voice roared behind him, but Abdullah didn’t stop. His feet pounded against the dirt as he raced toward the school gate, every instinct in his body screaming at him to run. He didn’t know where he was going—he just needed to get away.

Behind him, the sound of Mr. Jones yelling orders faded as he gained distance. He sprinted down the dirt path that led away from the school, heart pounding in his chest. He could hear the shouts of the boys on the porch, their voices carried by the wind. The last thing he caught was Mr. Jones shouting, "Get the bakkie!"

---


**The Chase Begins**

Abdullah ran harder, faster than he ever had before. The dry, dusty road stretched out ahead of him, and he focused on the familiar landscape, trying to keep his breathing steady. His mind was racing just as fast as his legs. He couldn’t go back—not after this. He needed to escape, to get away from the punishments, the rivalry, the pressure. For a fleeting moment, he imagined running all the way back to Congo, back to the life he knew before everything fell apart.

The sound of an engine roared in the distance, and Abdullah’s heart jumped in his chest. He glanced over his shoulder and saw Mr. Jones in the bakkie, the school’s pickup truck, barreling down the hill toward him. In the back of the truck, he could see Gerald, Morgan, Brian, and Richard, leaning over the edge, calling out and catcalling like they were watching a game.

Abdullah’s legs burned as he pushed himself faster, but the bakkie was closing in. He needed to get off the road, to find somewhere to hide. Up ahead, he spotted the narrow one-car bridge that crossed the river—a wooden bridge usually occupied by a herd of cows at this time of day. If he could just get across, the bakkie would have to stop.

---


**The Bridge Full of Cows**

The bridge came into view, and just as he’d hoped, it was packed with cows, their large bodies blocking the way. Abdullah darted toward the bridge, weaving through the cows, slipping between their slow, lumbering movements. He could hear the bakkie screech to a halt behind him as Mr. Jones slammed on the brakes.

"Move the cows!" Gerald shouted from the back of the truck, his voice filled with excitement.

"Come on, you stupid animals!" Morgan whistled and waved his arms, trying to herd the cows off the bridge. The boys in the back of the bakkie laughed, enjoying the chaos.

But while the others were focused on the cows, one of them wasn’t. Richard.

Richard didn’t wait. He leapt off the back of the bakkie and hit the ground running, his eyes locked on Abdullah. There was no hesitation in his movements, no mocking laughter. This wasn’t a game to him. He was chasing Abdullah for real, and Abdullah could feel him gaining ground.

---


**The Chase Tightens**

Abdullah’s lungs burned, and his legs felt like lead as he pushed himself harder. He was fast—he knew that—but Richard had the stamina for long distances. It was only a matter of time before he caught up. Every second, every step brought Richard closer.

"Come on!" Abdullah muttered to himself, glancing at the trees and bushes lining the road, searching for an escape. He needed to hide, to disappear. Vonnie’s spaza shop wasn’t far now. If he could just reach it, he could slip behind the shop and find somewhere to think, to plan his next move.

Twenty steps. Just twenty steps to freedom.

But then he heard the scream.

---


**Richard in Danger**

Abdullah’s heart skipped a beat as the scream pierced the air. He spun around, skidding to a stop. Richard was surrounded by three dogs—stray, vicious-looking animals, their teeth bared, growling and snapping at him. His shirt was off, and he was swinging it wildly, trying to fend them off, but it wasn’t working. The dogs were closing in, teeth flashing in the sun.

Fear froze Abdullah in place for a second. He could hear Richard yelling, panicking, but the dogs weren’t backing off. In that instant, everything between them—the rivalry, the anger, the taunts—faded away. Richard wasn’t his enemy right now. He was just a kid, terrified and cornered.

Without thinking, Abdullah grabbed a half-broken brick from the side of the road and flung it at one of the dogs. The brick hit the dog’s side with a dull thud, and the animal yelped, backing off for a moment. Abdullah didn’t stop. He rushed forward, kicking and shouting, swinging his arms at the other two dogs.

The dogs hesitated, unsure of the new threat, and Abdullah used the moment to push Richard toward the edge of the road. They stood back to back, panting, both boys terrified but determined. The dogs circled them, growling, but now that they were together, they stood their ground.

"Stay close," Abdullah muttered, his voice low.

Richard nodded, too out of breath to respond. His eyes were wide with fear, his shirt still clutched in one hand as a makeshift weapon. But together, they fought off the dogs, yelling and kicking until the animals finally retreated, their snarls fading into the distance.

---


**Brothers Instead of Rivals**

For a long moment, the two boys just stood there, breathing hard, shoulders touching, the adrenaline still pumping through their veins. They were bruised, dirty, but they had made it. Together.

Richard, still catching his breath, looked at Abdullah with something different in his eyes. There was no smirk, no arrogance. Just a quiet, unspoken respect.

He gave a small nod, almost imperceptible. "Thanks," he said, his voice hoarse.

Abdullah nodded back, feeling the weight of everything they’d been through lift slightly. He didn’t say anything. He didn’t need to. They both knew things had changed.

As they started walking back toward the school, the rivalry that had once burned so hot between them felt... distant. They weren’t enemies anymore. Maybe not friends yet, but something more like brothers. Brothers who had faced danger side by side.

And for the first time in weeks, Abdullah felt like he wasn’t running away.

---


**End of Chapter 9**
