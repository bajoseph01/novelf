## Chapter 3: Finding My Feet

***Tuesday, January 14th***
***Day two at Low Water Primary, and I already have a routine: dodge the chickens, jump the fence, and start the long walk to school. It's not so bad when you've got company, even if that company is a bunch of kids half your size with twice your energy.***

[Illustration suggestion: Abdullah leaping over a fence, with chickens scattering and kids cheering him on in the background.]

***Today, Berenice caught up with me again. She's got this way of smiling like she's in on a joke nobody else knows about. She asked me about Congo, and I told her a bit—how it's nothing like here, how I miss the music and the food. She just listened, nodding like she understood, even though I could tell this was all a world away for her.***

[Illustration suggestion: Berenice jogging next to Abdullah, who's talking and gesturing with his hands, a nostalgic look on his face.]

***In class, Mr. Jones handed out a math test like a morning greeting. I barely had time to write my name before the numbers started swimming in front of my eyes. Brian, though, he was scribbling away like it was Christmas and the test was his present.***

[Illustration suggestion: Abdullah staring blankly at a test paper, while Brian next to him writes with gusto, a determined glint in his eye.]

***At lunch, I sat with my new gang again. The sandwich guy, who'se name is Mervin, actually tried to eat those ten sandwiches (most of them from Brian’s lunch). He got to seven before he turned a shade of green I'd only seen on sick goats. The reading girl, Gwen, didnt even look up from her book, just passed him a napkin like shed seen it all before.***

[Illustration suggestion: Tom with a pile of sandwich crusts, looking queasy, while Sarah nonchalantly reads her book and offers a napkin without looking.]

***The twins, Gerald and Geraldine (yes, really), kept everyone entertained with their magic tricks. They're not half bad, unless you count Geraldines tendency to reveal the secret if you laugh at her jokes. I think they might be my favorite people here.***

[Illustration suggestion: Gerald pulling a coin from behind Geraldines ear, while she's about to spill the beans with a wide grin, and Abdullah chuckles.]

***After school, Berenice invited me to join their impromptu soccer game. I havent played in forever, but it was like riding a bike—you don't forget how to dodge and weave, even if the goats are your usual opponents.***

[Illustration suggestion: Abdullah in the middle of a makeshift soccer game, skillfully maneuvering the ball away from a lunging defender.]
***Turns out, I'm not half bad, and by the end of the game, even the kids whod been giving me side-eyes in the morning were slapping my back and saying, "Not bad for a newbie." It felt good, like I'd earned a little piece of respect on this strange new turf.***

[Illustration suggestion: Kids from the soccer game surrounding Abdullah, some giving thumbs up, others clapping him on the back.]

***As the sun dipped low and the shadows stretched out, I walked back to Uncle Jan's farm, the chatter and laughter of my new friends echoing in my mind. For the first time since I got here, I didnt feel quite so alone.***

[Illustration suggestion: Abdullah walking home with a soccer ball under his arm, a content smile on his face, as the sun sets behind the familiar silhouette of the farm.]

***Today was a good day. And maybe, just maybe, I'll have more of them here. Maybe I can carve out a slice of home in this new place, with these new people. It's not Congo, but it's starting to feel a little bit like mine.***

[Illustration suggestion: Abdullah looking back at the school in the distance, a hopeful gaze in his eyes, as the last light of day glows on the horizon.]

***As I walked, the laughter of my new friends still warming my heart, I realized something important. This place, with it's odd customs and unfamiliar faces, was slowly becoming a part of me. It wasnt just the soccer game or the jokes at lunch. It was the small moments, the nods of understanding, and the shared struggles in class that were weaving into the fabric of my daily life.***

[Illustration suggestion: Abdullah with a thoughtful expression, small moments from his day like a shared joke, a nod from Berenice, and a high-five from Brian, all floating around his head like memories.]

***When I finally saw Uncle Jan's farm on the horizon, the goats bleating their welcome, it didnt feel so foreign anymore. This farm, these animals, the dusty path I walked every day—they were becoming my new routine, my new normal.***

[Illustration suggestion: Abdullah approaching the farm, with goats in the foreground greeting him; the farm is bathed in the warm, golden light of the setting sun.]

***As I closed the gate behind me and the last light of the day faded, I felt a quiet sense of achievement. Today, I wasnt just the new kid from a far-off place. I was Abdullah, the soccer player, the friend, the part of something. And that thought alone made me eager to see what tomorrow would bring.***

[Illustration suggestion: Abdullah closing the farm gate, looking back at the path behind him, a determined and hopeful look on his face as the stars begin to twinkle in the twilight sky.]

***This place might never replace the home I'd lost, but it was offering me something precious—a chance to grow roots in new soil, to become part of a community, and to build a life that honored the memory of my parents. I went to bed that night with a smile, thinking, "Bring it on, Low Water Primary. I'm ready for you now."***

[Illustration suggestion: Abdullah lying in bed, a content smile on his face, the room dimly lit by a small bedside lamp, with a diary open next to him on the nightstand.]

***And with that thought, I drifted off to sleep, the sounds of the farm lulling me into dreams of tomorrows adventures.***
