## Chapter 2: The New Crew and the Classroom Encounter

***Monday, January 13th***

***The holidays are over, and I've definitely earned my stripes working on Uncle Jan's goat farm. I mean, who knew goats had so much... personality? And now, school's about to start. Greeaaat….***
[Illustration suggestion: Abdullah with a pitchfork, surrounded by goats, one of which is chewing on his shirt.]
***So there I am, setting off for Low Water Primary. It's a 7 km trek, and I'm not even kidding. By the time I reach the school gates, it feels like I've joined this parade of kids. They're all sizes and shapes, but next to me? They look like they're still in preschool.***

[Illustration suggestion: Abdullah towering over a line of much smaller kids, looking bewildered.]

***Then, out of the blue, there's this girl, Berenice. She's jogging up like it's no big deal, and you can tell she's one of those sporty types. She gives me this look, head to toe, with a smile that says, "You're not from around here, are you?" Feels like I'm on display at some exotic animal exhibit.***

[Illustration suggestion: Berenice sizing up Abdullah with a curious smile as he awkwardly tries to ignore the attention.]

***At school, things don't get any better. I'm like the main attraction at a circus. Girls are all giggles and whispers, and the boys? They've got this "who's the new guy?" look plastered all over their faces.***

***Uncle Jan, in his infinite wisdom, told me first things first: meet the principal. That guys a dead ringer for Mr. Miyagi from "The Karate Kid." I half-expected him to start giving me wax-on, wax-off lessons right there.***

[Illustration suggestion: Abdullah standing nervously in front of the principal's desk, while the principal, who looks like Mr. Miyagi, eyes him curiously.]

***Mr. Miyagi, I mean, the principal, marches me over to meet my Grade 6 teacher, Mr. Jones. Imagine a bear in a sweater, and you've got the right picture. He growls something about tucking in my shirt and not slouching, and I'm just nodding like one of those bobblehead dolls.***

***In the classroom, there's this kid in the back corner, all alone, scribbling away like he's trying to win some kind of homework marathon. Who even does that before the bell rings? He's got these bright, shiny shoes, looking like theyve never stepped in a puddle, let alone a goat pen…and here I’m standing with both big toes covered in dust!***

[Illustration suggestion: A studious kid with a focused expression, sitting at a desk piled with books, while others are playing around him.]

***And this kids not just a shiny-shoed wonder; everything about him screams brand new. It's like he's the human version of a fresh notebook at the start of the term.***
***Just when I thought I could slip out and avoid more of this first-day zoo, the shiny kid stands up. He's got so much Vaseline on his hair and face, I'm worried he might slip right out of his chair. He looks like a walking, talking Back to School advert.***

[Illustration suggestion: A boy with slick, shiny hair and a face so glossy it reflects light, standing confidently with a hand outstretched.]

***He walks over, hand out like he's running for class president or something, and hits me with, "Hi, I'm Brian, and yes, my dad*** **is *as dangerous as he looks."***

***I'm standing there, trying to figure out if that's a warning or a weird flex, but I shake his hand anyway. It's like shaking hands with a tub of margarine.***

[Illustration suggestion: Abdullah looking confused as he shakes a greasy-handed Brians hand, with a thought bubble that reads, "Should I be worried about his dad?"]

***Brian starts rattling on about how his dads the deputy disciplinarian or something,and how he can fix any bad choice with his special sticks, whatever that means.***
***I'm just nodding, still trying to figure out if I'm supposed to be impressed or scared.***
***The bell rings, and the classroom suddenly fills up like someone turned on the kid-tap. They're all taking seats, sneaking peeks at me and Brian, probably trying to suss out the new dynamics.***

[Illustration suggestion: Kids flooding into the classroom, with various expressions of curiosity and amusement directed at Abdullah and Brian.]

***Mr. Jones claps his hands for attention, and that's my cue to find a seat. The only one left is right next to Brian, go figure. So now I'm the new kid with the shiny sidekick. This is going to be an interesting year.***

[Illustration suggestion: Abdullah sitting awkwardly next to a grinning Brian, both surrounded by a sea of curious faces.]

***As Mr. Jones starts talking about the importance of good choices, hard work and dedication, I can't help but let my mind wander. I'm thinking about how I ended up here, in this strange new world that's so different from my old life. I'm wondering if I'll ever fit in, if I'll ever understand the rules of this place, or if I'll always be the odd one out.***

[Illustration suggestion: Abdullah daydreaming at his desk; a thought bubble shows a montage of his old home, the journey to his uncles, and the faces of his new classmates.]

***But then I catch Berenices eye from across the room, and she gives me a thumbs-up. It's a small gesture, but it feels like a lifeline. Maybe, just maybe, I won't have to navigate this all on my own.***

[Illustration suggestion: Berenice giving Abdullah a supportive thumbs-up from across the room, with Abdullah looking surprised but grateful.]

***Lunchtime rolls around, and I find myself sitting with Brian and a few other kids who don't seem to mind the newness oozing off of me. They're a ragtag bunch: there's a quiet girl who's always reading, a guy who claims he can eat ten sandwiches in one go, and a pair of twins who finish each others sentences.***

[Illustration suggestion: A diverse group of kids sitting at a lunch table, with Abdullah in the middle looking a bit overwhelmed by his chatty new friends.]

***We're the misfits, the leftovers, the ones who don't quite fit into the neat little boxes of school social life. But as we share stories and laugh at Brians dad jokes, I start to feel like maybe I've found my gang. They're weird and wonderful, and they don't care that I'm the tall African kid with an accent.***

[Illustration suggestion: The group laughing together, with Abdullah starting to smile genuinely for the first time.]

***By the end of the day, my farm brain is exhausted but hopeful. This place is nothing like home, and I miss my parents more than ever. But I've got a feeling that these people, this odd little community, might just become something like a family to me.***

[Illustration suggestion: Abdullah walking home from school, a small smile on his face, the sun setting behind him, casting a long shadow ahead.]
