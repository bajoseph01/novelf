## Chapter 10: A Fight for Survival, Not Rivalry

---


Abdullah and Richard stood still for a moment, their bodies tense, chests heaving with exhaustion after driving the dogs away. The dust settled around them, and the snarls of the dogs faded into the distance, but their hearts still pounded from the chaos of the last few minutes.

Abdullah could hardly believe it. Just moments ago, he had been running from Richard and everything that represented his new life. Now, they were standing back to back, having just fought off three vicious dogs together. The rivalry that had burned so hot between them felt distant, replaced by something neither of them could quite define. They were no longer enemies.

Richard was the first to move, wiping the sweat off his forehead and shaking his head in disbelief. That was insane, he muttered, his voice still hoarse from the chase and the fight.

Abdullah, still catching his breath, nodded. His legs ached from running, and his muscles throbbed from the adrenaline. Yeah, was all he could manage to say.

For a moment, they stood in silence, each processing what had just happened. The tension between them, the chase, the dogs—it was all swirling in their minds, but something had changed. The edge that used to be between them had softened.

Richard turned toward Abdullah, eyes no longer filled with competition but with something closer to respect. He gave a quick nod, a silent acknowledgment of what they’d just been through together. Thanks… for coming back, Richard said, still sounding unsure of himself.

Abdullah shrugged. You would’ve done the same, he replied, though neither of them was entirely sure if that was true.

---


**The Journey Back**

The adrenaline was fading now, and with it came a dull ache in Abdullah’s legs. His shirt clung to his back, soaked with sweat, and the sun felt hotter than ever as it beat down on the dirt road. Without a word, the two boys began walking back toward the school. They moved slowly at first, each step heavy with exhaustion, but the silence between them wasn’t uncomfortable—it was a shared understanding that something had shifted.

As they walked, Abdullah’s mind wandered back to what had happened just before the dogs attacked. The chase had been about proving something—Richard trying to show that he was better, faster, stronger. And Abdullah had been running not just from Richard, but from the frustration, the anger, the feeling of always being on the outside.

But now? Now things were different.

After a few minutes of walking, Richard broke the silence. You’re fast, he said, almost begrudgingly. But you burn out quick.

Abdullah let out a small laugh. And you can’t sprint if your life depended on it.

Richard gave a tired grin. I guess I’ve got more stamina. His voice held no malice now, just an observation. For the first time, it wasn’t a competition—it was just a fact.

They continued walking, the tension between them gone. Abdullah glanced over at Richard and caught a glimpse of the boy who had been his rival for so long, but now he just saw someone who had been as scared as he was. And for the first time since he arrived in South Africa, he didn’t feel like he was alone.

As they approached the school, the bakkie came into view. The cows had cleared off the bridge, and Mr. Jones stood with his hands on his hips, glaring in their direction. The other boys were still hanging around the bakkie, but their laughter had died down. They were watching now—watching as Abdullah and Richard walked back together.

---


**Facing Mr. Jones**

Abdullah and Richard both stopped a few meters away from Mr. Jones, the weight of what they had done settling back into their minds. There was no doubt in their minds that they were in for it. The entire school had seen Abdullah run, and now they were returning after who knew how long, bruised, dirty, and clearly having been in trouble.

Mr. Jones didn’t say anything at first. He just looked at them—really looked—and seemed to see that something was different. His mouth was set in a thin line, but his eyes flickered between Abdullah and Richard, as if trying to figure out what had just happened between them.

Inside. Now, he finally said, his voice low but firm.

Abdullah’s stomach dropped, and for a moment, he thought about running again. But instead, he gave a quick nod and followed Richard inside, feeling the weight of the other boys’ eyes on his back.

As they walked through the doors of the school, Mr. Jones followed close behind, his heavy footsteps echoing through the quiet hallways. The rest of the school must have been watching from the classroom windows, waiting to see what punishment awaited them.

Mr. Jones ushered them into his office, where the familiar wooden desk stood like a barrier between them and the impending consequences. Sit, he ordered.

Abdullah sank into the chair, his mind racing. He couldn’t shake the feeling that everything was about to fall apart again, but there was something different this time. He wasn’t alone. Richard sat next to him, quiet and composed, no longer the smug boy from earlier.

---


**The Unexpected Turn**

Mr. Jones leaned back in his chair, eyes scanning both boys with a look that made it clear he was trying to make sense of the situation. He hadn’t expected them to come back together, not like this.

You two, Mr. Jones began, shaking his head slightly. I’ve been dealing with this nonsense between you for weeks. Running off like that… fighting dogs in the street… What’s next? What do I have to do to get through to you?

Richard and Abdullah exchanged a glance, both knowing that they had crossed a line today. But before either of them could respond, Mr. Jones continued.

You both could’ve gotten seriously hurt today. And for what? To prove something? To settle some ridiculous score?

The boys were silent. There was no point in arguing.

Mr. Jones sighed, rubbing his temples. I’m not going to punish you the way I should. Not today. You’ve clearly been through enough. He paused, looking at them closely. But I expect things to change between you two. This… whatever this rivalry has been, it ends now. Understood?

Richard nodded immediately, his expression serious. Yes, sir.

Abdullah followed suit, a weight lifting off his shoulders. Yes, sir.

Mr. Jones eyed them both for another long moment before finally sitting back in his chair. Go home. And don’t let me see you in here again.

---


**A New Beginning**

The walk home was quiet but not uncomfortable. The rivalry between Abdullah and Richard felt like a distant memory now, replaced by something else—something more like mutual respect. They weren’t suddenly best friends, but there was an unspoken understanding between them. They had been through something together, and it had changed everything.

As they parted ways at the school gate, Richard gave Abdullah a small wave, the tension between them completely gone. See you tomorrow, Dullah.

Yeah, see you, Abdullah replied, feeling lighter than he had in weeks.

As he walked home, his mind wandered back to what Mr. Jones had said. The rivalry was over, but that didn’t mean the challenges were. He still had to figure out who he was in this new world, still had to find his place in South Africa. But now, for the first time, he felt like he could face it—whatever it was.

He was no longer running away. He was standing his ground.

---


**End of Chapter 10**
