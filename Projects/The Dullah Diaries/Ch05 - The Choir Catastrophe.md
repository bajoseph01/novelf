## Chapter 5: The Choir Catastrophe

*Friday, February 14th*

Choir practice. Just when I thought school couldnt get any weirder, they decided to throw singing into the mix. I shuffled into the music room, which smelled faintly of old books and chalk—a stark contrast to the open air I'm used to. The younger kids buzzed around like excited bees, their voices already warming up. I felt like a giant among them, sticking out like a sore thumb.

"Abdullah, back row with the taller boys!" Mrs. Llucing snapped, pointing her ever-present ruler toward the bench. Her eyes were sharp behind her tiny glasses, and I got the feeling she didnt tolerate nonsense.

I made my way to the back, squeezing between Gerald and Mervin. The bench wobbled under our combined weight. Mrs. Llucing tapped her music stand. "Attention, everyone! From the top!"

We launched into some song about rivers and mountains. I mumbled along, trying to be invisible. But then came a high note that everyone else seemed to hit effortlessly. I took a deep breath and went for it.

My voice cracked so loudly it echoed off the walls. Silence fell. Then, like a dam breaking, the back row erupted into laughter. My cheeks burned hotter than the sun back home.

"Nice one," Gerald snickered, nudging me. Mortified, I shifted my weight, accidentally knocking into him. Everything seemed to go in slow motion as Gerald flailed and toppled off the bench, landing with a thud.

"Hey! Watch it!" Richard snapped, stepping forward to help Gerald up. His eyes met mine, a storm brewing. "You did that on purpose!"

"It was an accident," I protested, my heart pounding.

"Sure didnt look like it," he retorted, squaring up to me. The room had shrunk; all eyes were on us.

"Enough!" Mrs. Llucings voice sliced through the tension. She marched over, ruler in hand, her face a mix of fury and exasperation. "I've had it with you Grade Sixes! Out! Both of you! Go find Mr. Jones immediately!"

I glanced at Richard, who'se glare hadnt softened. Without another word, we shuffled out of the room, the door closing behind us with a decisive click.

The hallway was quiet except for the distant hum of other classes. My stomach twisted. Facing Mr. Jones was the last thing I wanted to do.

"Great job, newbie," Richard muttered, not bothering to look at me.

I clenched my fists but said nothing. Arguing wouldnt help. Besides, a part of me knew this was bigger than a choir mishap. This was about everything—Athletics Day, Berenice, being the new kid who didnt know his place.

We walked in tense silence toward Mr. Jones office, each step heavier than the last. I could feel the weight of the upcoming confrontation pressing down on me.

As we reached the door, I took a deep breath, trying to steady myself. This wasnt how I imagined my day going. But then again, nothing since arriving here had been as I'd imagined.

Richard knocked, and a gruff voice beckoned us inside. Time to face the music—pun intended.
